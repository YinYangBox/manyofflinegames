Settings

The short aiming line shows your direction. It does not predict the entire shot.

Rules

House eight-ball. Pot your seven balls, then legally pot the black eight to win. Solids are 1–7; stripes are 9–15. The white cue ball is never a scoring ball.

Controls. Move the mouse to aim. Press on the table, drag backward along the cue and release to shoot. On mobile, touch toward your target, pull back and release. A longer pull gives more power. Release with very little power to cancel, or use Cancel shot. In portrait orientation the table rotates to fit your screen.

Break and groups. You break first. Groups are assigned after the break, by the first solid or stripe you legally pot. The table stays open after a break, even if balls are potted. The eight is spotted back on the table if it falls on the break. Any numbered ball may be hit first on the break.

Legal shots. On an open table, hit a solid or stripe first. Once groups are assigned, hit your own group first. You may only hit the eight first when your group was already cleared before the shot. After first contact, a ball must touch a cushion or an object ball must be potted. These same cushion-or-pot requirements apply to the break.

Turns and fouls. Keep shooting when you legally pot one of your balls (or any solid or stripe on the break). Otherwise the turn changes. Scratching, missing every ball, hitting the wrong group first, or failing the cushion-or-pot rule is a foul. The opponent gets ball in hand: tap a clear spot on the cloth to place the white ball.

The eight. Outside the break, potting the eight before clearing your group, or potting it during a foul, loses the rack. Clearing your final group ball and the eight in the same shot also loses. Any pocket is allowed; calling pockets is not required in these house rules.

Credits. Against the CPU, each of your legally potted scoring balls earns 8 / 12 / 18 credits on Easy / Normal / Hard. A win adds 70 / 110 / 170 credits. Break pots also earn credits. Fouls, opponent balls and local two-player matches give no credits. Restarting or leaving keeps credits already earned. Skins never change physics.

CPU. Easy aims less accurately and considers fewer shots. Normal plans direct pots. Hard examines all legal direct pots and has steadier aim. Every difficulty uses the same ball physics. This is a top-down arcade simulation without jump shots or cue-ball spin controls.
